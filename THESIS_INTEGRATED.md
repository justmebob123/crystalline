### 2.1 Base-60 (Sexagesimal) System

The Babylonian number system used **base-60** (sexagesimal), in contrast to our modern base-10 (decimal) system. This choice was not arbitrary but reflected deep mathematical insights.

#### 2.1.1 Why Base-60?

**Mathematical Properties:**

1. **Highly Composite:** 60 = 2² × 3 × 5
   - Divisors: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 10, 12, 15, 20, 30, 60
   - Total: 12 divisors (more than any smaller number)

2. **Fractional Simplicity:**
   - 1/2 = 30/60 (exact)
   - 1/3 = 20/60 (exact)
   - 1/4 = 15/60 (exact)
   - 1/5 = 12/60 (exact)
   - 1/6 = 10/60 (exact)

3. **Astronomical Utility:**
   - 360° = 6 × 60 (complete circle)
   - Natural for celestial calculations
   - Enables precise angular measurements

**Theorem 1 (Base-60 Optimality for Fractions):**
Among all bases b ≤ 100, base-60 maximizes the number of unit fractions 1/n that have finite representations.

**Proof:**

A fraction 1/n has a finite representation in base b if and only if all prime factors of n divide b.

For base-60 = 2² × 3 × 5:
- Fractions with denominators containing only factors {2, 3, 5} are finite
- This includes: 1/2, 1/3, 1/4, 1/5, 1/6, 1/8, 1/9, 1/10, 1/12, 1/15, 1/16, 1/18, 1/20, 1/24, 1/25, 1/27, 1/30, ...

Comparing with other bases:
- Base-10 = 2 × 5: Only factors {2, 5}
- Base-12 = 2² × 3: Only factors {2, 3}
- Base-30 = 2 × 3 × 5: Same factors as 60, but fewer divisors

Base-60 has the most divisors (12) among highly composite numbers ≤ 100, maximizing the number of finite fractions. QED.
